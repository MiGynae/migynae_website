Water Breaking, Urine or Discharge? Know the Difference

In late pregnancy it can be hard to tell what is happening. Three things get confused: leaking waters, urine, and normal discharge. Here is how to tell them apart — and what to do next.
1

Leaking waters vs urine vs discharge

Leaking waters Urine Discharge
Colour Clear or pale straw, may have white flecks Yellow White or milky
Smell Odourless or mild — not urine-like Ammonia / urine smell Mild or none
Feel Thin, watery Watery Thick, sticky
Flow Trickle or gush you cannot stop; keeps wetting the pad Small; leaks on cough or laugh; you can hold it Small, steady; does not soak the pad
What to do Could be your waters — call your doctor / go in Common; pelvic-floor (Kegel) exercises help Normal, unless itch, smell or colour change

Not sure? Put on a clean pad and check after an hour. If it keeps getting wet with clear fluid, treat it as leaking waters and contact your doctor.

2

True labour vs false labour

True labour False labour (Braxton Hicks)
Timing Regular; contractions come closer together over time Irregular; do not come closer together
Strength Get stronger and more intense over time Stay mild, or fade away
On moving Continue — may even get stronger Often ease with rest or a position change
Where Start in the back, wrap around to the front Felt mostly in the front or lower tummy only
With it May have a "show" (mucus plug) or leaking waters No other changes

When pains feel regular, start timing them. If they keep coming closer and stronger, or your waters go — head to the hospital.

3

Track contractions and kicks in the app

  • Use the contraction tracker — tap at the start and end of each pain and it shows you the pattern
  • Use the kick tracker to count movements daily — a drop in movement needs immediate medical attention
  • Get doctor-backed guidance on discharge in pregnancy and what colour changes to watch for
  • Week-by-week guidance on what to expect in the third trimester
Go in now if you have
Do not wait if you notice any of the following:
  • A gush or steady trickle of clear fluid that keeps coming (your waters)
  • Regular, strong contractions coming closer together
  • Any vaginal bleeding
  • Your baby moving less than usual
  • Green or foul-smelling fluid
