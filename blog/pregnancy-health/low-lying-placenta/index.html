Low-Lying Placenta: What It Means and What to Do

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Understand your reports, without the worry - MiGynae App

Scan explainers, week-by-week tracking and doctor-backed guidance in one place.

A low-lying placenta sounds scary, but it is common and usually settles on its own. Here is what it means, what to watch for, and when to get help.

What it means

The placenta is your baby's lifeline. Low-lying means it has settled in the lower part of the uterus, near or over the cervix (the opening). It is usually found on a routine scan - not because anything is wrong.

The good news first

A low placenta at the 18-22 week scan is very common. As the uterus grows, it usually moves up and away on its own by the third trimester. Only a small number stay low - so a low report early on is rarely a reason to worry.

What to do - and avoid

Do:

  • Go for your repeat scan (usually around 32 weeks) to recheck the position.

Rest more, and keep your doctor's number handy.

Keep all your scan reports together.

Avoid (if your doctor advises):

  • Heavy lifting and hard exercise
  • Intercourse
  • Long travel, especially later in pregnancy
  • Ignoring even a small amount of bleeding

Your doctor will tell you how careful to be, based on how low it is.

Track your baby, and your body

Two free tools in the MiGynae App help you here. Use the kick tracker to keep an eye on your baby's movements. And use the contraction tracker - with a low placenta, tightenings or contractions can set off bleeding, so if you feel any, time them and head in. Keep both handy.

Go to the hospital at once if you have

The main warning sign is bleeding - do not wait:

  • Any vaginal bleeding, even painless or light
  • Bright red bleeding, with or without clots
  • Tightening, cramps or belly pain
  • Reduced baby movements
  • Feeling very dizzy or faint

About your delivery

If the placenta moves up in time, a normal delivery is usually possible. If it stays low and covers the opening near your due date, your doctor will plan a caesarean for safety. This is a planned, protective choice - not a failure.

Questions to ask your doctor

So you feel clear and calm:

  • How low is my placenta, and is it covering the opening?
  • What activities should I avoid right now?
  • When is my repeat scan?
  • What should I do if I notice any bleeding?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a low-lying placenta (placenta previa)?
A low-lying placenta occurs when the placenta attaches in the lower portion of the uterus, lying within 2 centimeters of the internal cervical os (opening) or partially/completely covering it.
Will a low-lying placenta move up as pregnancy progresses?
Yes, in more than 90% of cases diagnosed at the 18–20 week anomaly scan, the placenta migrates upward and away from the cervix as the upper uterine muscular wall expands into the third trimester.
What precautions must I take if diagnosed with a low-lying placenta?
Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous physical workouts, vaginal intercourse (pelvic rest), and prolonged bumpy journeys. Ensure you rest adequately and keep your doctor's emergency contact accessible.
What is the primary danger sign of placenta previa?
Sudden, painless, bright red vaginal bleeding in the second or third trimester. Even a slight streak of red blood or spotting requires immediate emergency hospital assessment.
Can I have a normal delivery if my placenta remains low-lying?
If the placenta remains within 2 centimeters of the cervix or covers the internal opening at term (36–37 weeks), a planned caesarean section is required for maternal and fetal safety to prevent severe intrapartum hemorrhage.
