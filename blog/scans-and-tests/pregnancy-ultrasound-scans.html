Pregnancy Scans: Your Ultrasound Guide, Week by Week

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Understand every scan and report - MiGynae App

Week-by-week tracking, scan explainers and doctor-backed guidance in one place.

Scans are one of the happiest parts of pregnancy, and one of the most asked about. Here is which scan happens when, and what each one checks.

The main scans in pregnancy

You will have a few key scans through pregnancy. Each one has a purpose and a right time - here is what to expect.

Dating scan · 6-9 weeks: Confirms the pregnancy, its location, the heartbeat, and your due date.

NT scan · 11-13+6 weeks: Part of early screening for Down syndrome and some conditions.

Anomaly (TIFFA) scan · 18-22 weeks: A detailed check of baby's organs, growth and the placenta.

Growth scan · 28-34 weeks: Checks baby's growth, the fluid, the placenta and blood flow.

Your doctor may advise extra scans depending on your pregnancy. Always follow your own plan.

Are scans safe?

Yes. Ultrasound uses sound waves, not radiation (not X-rays), and is considered safe for you and your baby when done for medical reasons. There is no special risk in the scans your doctor advises.

How to prepare

For early scans, you may be asked to come with a full bladder - drink water and do not pass urine just before.

In very early pregnancy, a transvaginal scan (internal probe) may be done - it is safe and gives a clearer picture.

Wear loose, comfortable clothing for easy belly access.

A cool gel is put on your tummy - it may feel cold, nothing more.

Carry your previous scans and reports every time.

One important thing in India

The law:

  • By law (the PCPNDT Act), the baby's sex is never told during any scan in India. This protects girls. Please do not ask your doctor or sonographer they cannot and will not share it. A healthy baby is the real good news.

If something needs a closer look

Sometimes a scan shows a "soft marker" or asks for a repeat. This often turns out to be nothing. Do not panic from an online search - let your own doctor explain what it means for you.

Good questions to ask

At your scan visit:

  • Is my baby's growth on track for my weeks?
  • Is the fluid and placenta normal?
  • Do I need any further or repeat scan?
  • When is my next scan due?

Frequently Asked Questions

Are ultrasound scans safe for me and my baby during pregnancy?
Yes. Diagnostic pregnancy ultrasounds use high-frequency sound waves rather than ionizing radiation (like X-rays). When performed by trained medical professionals for clinical purposes, ultrasound has an exceptional safety record.
Why do I need a full bladder for early pregnancy scans?
A full bladder tilts the uterus upward into the pelvic cavity and pushes gas-filled intestines away, creating an optimal acoustic window that gives much clearer images during early transabdominal scans.
What is the difference between an NT scan and an anomaly (TIFFA) scan?
The NT scan (11 to 13+6 weeks) measures nuchal translucency to assess risks for chromosomal conditions like Down syndrome. The TIFFA anomaly scan (18 to 22 weeks) is a detailed structural evaluation of baby's organs, limbs, spine, brain, and placenta position.
Is sex determination allowed during pregnancy scans in India?
No. Under India's Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act, revealing or asking for the gender of the fetus is strictly prohibited by law and carries heavy legal penalties. Sonographers are barred from disclosing baby sex.
What happens if a scan report shows a soft marker or repeat requirement?
Many minor scan findings or 'soft markers' are harmless anatomical variations that resolve spontaneously. Do not panic from online searches; always let your obstetrician review the full report and advise appropriate follow-up.
