PCOD vs PCOS: What's the Difference?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Spot irregular periods early - MiGynae App

A free period tracker, symptom tracking and doctor-backed guidance.

PCOS is one of the most common hormone conditions in women, and it is often missed. Here is when to get yourself checked.

PCOD and PCOS are not the same thing

PCOD: the ovaries carry many small, immature follicles and periods can turn irregular. It is common and often mild.

PCOS: PCOD along with a wider set of hormone symptoms - like acne, extra hair, weight gain and irregular cycles. It is a fuller condition that needs closer care.

In short: PCOS is PCOD plus symptoms. Both are common, and both are manageable.

Common signs of PCOS to watch for

Irregular, late or missing periods

Acne, or oily skin that won't settle

Extra hair on the face, chin or body

Thinning hair on the scalp

Weight gain, or trouble losing weight around the tummy

Dark, velvety patches on the neck or underarms

Track your periods to catch the first sign

Irregular periods are often the earliest clue to PCOS - but they are easy to miss. The MiGynae App has a free period tracker that shows your pattern clearly. Use it, so you notice an irregular cycle long before it becomes a worry.

When to get yourself checked

If a few of these signs show up together, or your periods stay irregular beyond the first couple of years, see a gynaecologist. A simple check - your history, a scan and a few blood tests - is all it takes to know. The earlier you find out, the easier it is to manage.

Bring your tracked history along

Before your visit, open your cycle record in the MiGynae App period tracker and show it to your doctor. A clear history of your periods helps them understand you faster. Keep using the tracker every month.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the primary difference between PCOD and PCOS?
PCOD (Polycystic Ovarian Disease) is an anatomical condition where ovaries release partially mature eggs that turn into cysts, primarily manageable with lifestyle. PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) is a broader metabolic and endocrine disorder involving significant hormonal imbalance, elevated androgens, and systemic metabolic risks.
Is PCOD more common than PCOS?
Yes. PCOD is very common among young women, often mild, and frequently detected incidentally on an ultrasound. PCOS is less common but more severe, requiring closer medical and endocrine supervision.
Can women with PCOD or PCOS get pregnant naturally?
Yes. With proper cycle tracking, balanced nutrition, weight management, and medical assistance to induce ovulation if needed, the majority of women with PCOD and PCOS conceive and enjoy healthy pregnancies.
What diagnostic tests are used to detect PCOS?
A doctor typically assesses your clinical history, performs a pelvic ultrasound to examine ovarian morphology, and orders blood tests for fasting glucose/insulin, LH, FSH, thyroid profile (TSH), prolactin, and free testosterone.
Why does PCOS cause acne and facial hair?
Elevated insulin levels in PCOS stimulate the ovaries and adrenal glands to produce excess male hormones (androgens like testosterone), which overstimulate oil glands causing stubborn acne and cause hirsutism (coarse hair on chin, upper lip, or abdomen).
