PCOS Diet & Exercise: Small Steps for PCOS and PCOD

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

You can manage PCOS well with a few daily habits. Here are the basics - the full plan is in the app.

What to eat with PCOS

Choose whole grains and millets over white rice and maida

Fill half your plate with vegetables and fibre

Add protein to every meal - dal, eggs, paneer, sprouts

Cut sugary drinks, sweets and fried food

Do not skip meals, eat at regular times

How to stay active with PCOS

Aim for about 30 minutes of activity most days

Brisk walking, cycling or dancing all count

Add simple strength work or yoga twice a week

Consistency matters more than intensity

A few more habits that help

Even a small, steady weight loss can help regularise periods

Sleep 7 to 8 hours, and manage stress

Watch your cycle so you can see your progress

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best Indian diet for managing PCOS and PCOD?
A diet rich in high-fiber complex carbohydrates (millets, oats, whole wheat), plant and lean protein (moong dal, sprouts, paneer, eggs), and healthy fats, while minimizing refined flour (maida), white sugar, packaged snacks, and fried foods.
What kind of exercise works best for PCOS weight loss?
A combination of 30 minutes of moderate aerobic activity (brisk walking, cycling, or dancing) 4–5 days a week, plus 2 days of strength training or yoga to improve peripheral insulin sensitivity and metabolic rate.
Can diet and exercise cure PCOS permanently?
PCOS is a chronic metabolic condition that cannot be cured with a one-time pill, but clinical studies prove that adopting consistent dietary and movement habits can fully manage symptoms, restore ovulatory cycles, and prevent long-term complications.
How does sleep and stress management help in PCOS?
Chronic stress elevates cortisol and worsens insulin resistance, which directly drives up androgen (male hormone) levels. Getting 7 to 8 hours of deep sleep and practicing mindfulness keeps stress hormones in check.
Do I need to eliminate dairy completely for PCOS?
No, unless you are lactose intolerant. Moderate intake of fermented or probiotic dairy like fresh plain curd (dahi) or paneer provides essential calcium and protein without aggravating PCOS for most women.
