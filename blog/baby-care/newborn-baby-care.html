Newborn Baby Care: Bringing Your Baby Home

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Just brought your baby home? The MiGynae App is with you

Feeding, sleep, baby tracking and doctor-backed newborn care in one place.

Your first days at home with your newborn. Here is how to hold, feed and care for your baby safely.

Holding your baby

Always support the head and neck when you lift or hold.

Wash or sanitise your hands before touching the baby.

Handle gently, and never shake your baby, even in play.

Skin-to-skin on your chest keeps the baby calm and warm.

Watch how it's done

First time holding a newborn? The MiGynae App has short, simple videos - how to hold and handle your baby, and how to swaddle - so you can do it with confidence. Explore them in the app.

Feeding

Breastfeed on demand, usually every 2 to 3 hours, day and night.

Let the baby feed until they let go and look settled.

Burp the baby after each feed to bring up wind.

6 or more wet nappies a day is a good sign of enough milk.

Safe sleep

Always lay the baby on the back to sleep, never the tummy.

Use a firm flat surface, with no pillows, soft toys or loose bedding.

Keep the baby's cot in your room for the first few months.

Never sleep with the baby on a sofa or under heavy blankets.

Warmth and cord care

Dress the baby in one more layer than you are wearing, not more.

Check warmth by feeling the chest or back, not the hands or feet.

Keep the umbilical stump clean and dry, and let it fall off on its own.

Fold the nappy below the stump, and put nothing on it unless advised.

Keep infections away

Limit visitors in the early weeks.

Everyone should wash hands before holding the baby.

Keep anyone unwell away, and do not let people kiss the baby.

Clean the nappy area front to back at every change.

Trust yourself

You will learn your baby's cues quickly. Rest when the baby rests, and ask for help, you are not meant to do this alone. If anything ever worries you, see the companion guide on newborn danger signs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should I hold and support my newborn baby?
Always support the baby's head and neck with your hand or arm whenever lifting, holding, or carrying. Newborn neck muscles are weak and cannot support the heavy head on their own for several months.
What are safe sleep guidelines for a newborn?
Always lay your baby flat on their back on a firm mattress in a separate crib or bassinet in your room. Avoid pillows, quilts, soft toys, loose blankets, and sleeping together on a couch or armchair to prevent suffocation and SIDS.
How do I care for the umbilical cord stump at home?
Keep the stump clean and dry at all times. Fold the top of the diaper down below the cord to expose it to air. Do not apply oil, turmeric, powders, or antiseptics unless specifically instructed by your paediatrician.
How many layers of clothes should a newborn wear?
Dress your baby in one more layer of clothing than you are comfortably wearing for the room temperature. Feel their chest or upper back to gauge warmth; hands and feet naturally feel cooler due to developing circulation.
How often should a newborn baby be bathed?
Give gentle sponge baths until the umbilical cord stump has naturally fallen off and healed. Thereafter, a gentle warm water bath 2 to 3 times a week is sufficient, as daily soaping can dry out sensitive newborn skin.
