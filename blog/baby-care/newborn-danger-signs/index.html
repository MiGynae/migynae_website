Newborn Danger Signs You Should Never Miss

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Keep newborn danger signs at your fingertips - MiGynae App

Danger signs, feeding help and doctor-backed newborn guidance, always with you.

Newborns can become unwell quickly. Knowing these signs, and acting fast, can save your baby's life. Trust your instinct.

Go to a doctor now if your baby has

Any one of these needs urgent medical care:

  • Trouble breathing fast or noisy breathing, chest pulling in, grunting, or blue lips or tongue
  • Will not feed, or feeds very poorly
  • Very sleepy, floppy, or hard to wake
  • Fits or convulsions
  • Fever, or body cold to the touch
  • Deep yellow skin, or yellow palms and soles
  • Red, swollen, foul-smelling or pus-draining belly button
  • Constant vomiting, a swollen hard tummy, or no urine or stool
  • A weak, high-pitched, or non-stop cry

See exactly what to watch for

Danger signs are easier to recognise when you have seen them. The MiGynae App has short videos on each newborn danger sign - what it looks like and how to handle it - for much better clarity. If you want to be sure, watch them in the app.

What to do right away

Do not wait and watch. Take the baby to your doctor or nearest hospital at once.

Keep the baby warm on the way, skin-to-skin against your chest.

Carry any hospital papers, and call ahead if you can.

Do not give any home remedy or medicine your doctor has not advised.

Until you reach the doctor

  • Hard breathing or blue lips: hold the baby upright against your chest, gently clear the nose, and rush.
  • Fitting or convulsing: lay the baby on their side, put nothing in the mouth, and note how long it lasts.
  • Cold to touch: warm slowly with skin-to-skin and a cap, and cover you both.
  • Feverish: do not wrap tightly, keep the baby cool and comfortable.
  • Awake and able to suck: keep offering breastmilk. Never force-feed a baby who is fitting or very drowsy.

When in doubt, get it checked

You know your baby best. It is always better to see a doctor and be reassured than to wait. With a newborn, never feel shy about asking for help.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most urgent danger signs in a newborn infant?
Fast breathing (>60 breaths/min), chest indrawing, grunting, blue lips or tongue, refusal to feed, convulsions or fits, fever (>100.4°F/38°C) or cold body (<97.7°F/36.5°C), deep yellow palms/soles, and extreme floppiness. Any single sign requires immediate emergency hospital care.
What should I do if my baby stops breathing or turns bluish?
Immediately hold the infant upright against your chest, gently clear any obstruction from the mouth/nose, stimulate the baby by rubbing their back or feet, and rush directly to the nearest emergency paediatric hospital.
When is jaundice in a newborn considered an emergency?
While mild jaundice on day 3–5 is common, jaundice appearing within the first 24 hours of birth, or spreading to the abdomen, palms, and soles of the feet with dark urine and pale stools, is dangerous and requires urgent phototherapy evaluation.
Why is a low body temperature dangerous for a newborn?
Hypothermia in newborns causes rapid energy depletion, low blood sugar (hypoglycemia), and breathing distress. If baby feels cold to touch, initiate skin-to-skin kangaroo care immediately while heading to a doctor.
What are the signs of an infected umbilical cord stump?
Redness or swelling spreading to the surrounding abdominal skin, foul-smelling pus oozing from the base, or continuous bleeding. This condition (omphalitis) requires urgent paediatric antibiotics.
