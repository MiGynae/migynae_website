Irregular Periods: When Your Period Isn't Normal

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Track your cycle the smart way - MiGynae App

Your period is a monthly health check. Here is what is normal, what is not, and when to see a doctor.

What a normal period looks like

Cycles usually come every 21 to 35 days, and bleeding lasts about 2 to 7 days. In the first year or two after periods begin, some irregularity is completely normal.

Signs your period needs a doctor

See a doctor if you have:

  • Very heavy bleeding soaking a pad every 1 to 2 hours, or large clots
  • Periods lasting longer than 7 days
  • Cycles under 21 or over 35 days, again and again
  • No period for 3 months or more (and you are not pregnant)
  • Bleeding between periods or after sex
  • Pain so bad it stops your daily life
  • No periods at all by age 15

Simple home remedies for period problems

Place a warm bag on your lower tummy for cramps

Stay hydrated, and sip warm ginger or ajwain water

Keep moving - a walk or gentle yoga eases cramps

Rest and sleep well around your period

Eat iron-rich foods (green veg, dates, jaggery) if your flow is heavy

Cut back on very salty and sugary food before your period

These ease mild symptoms. The warning signs above still need a doctor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What menstrual cycle length is considered normal?
A normal menstrual cycle typically ranges between 21 and 35 days, with bleeding lasting 2 to 7 days. Occasional shifts of 2–3 days due to stress or routine changes are common and normal.
How do I know if my period is abnormally heavy?
Bleeding is abnormally heavy (menorrhagia) if you soak through one or more sanitary pads every 1 to 2 hours, pass clots larger than a coin, or feel unusually weak, pale, or dizzy during your period.
Can stress cause periods to be late or irregular?
Yes. Severe emotional or physical stress triggers high levels of cortisol, which disrupts the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis and can suppress or delay ovulation, leading to late or missed periods.
When should I see a gynaecologist for irregular periods?
Consult a doctor if your cycles are consistently under 21 or over 35 days, you have missed periods for 3 consecutive months (and are not pregnant), bleed between periods, or have severe pelvic pain.
What is the difference between occasional irregular periods and PCOS?
Occasional irregularity is usually temporary and triggered by illness, weight changes, or stress. PCOS is a chronic hormonal and metabolic condition characterized by persistent irregular periods, cystic ovaries, acne, and excess androgen symptoms.
