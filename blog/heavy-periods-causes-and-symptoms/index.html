Heavy Periods (Menorrhagia): Causes, Symptoms & When to See a Doctor

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MD (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

What Constitutes Abnormally Heavy Menstrual Bleeding?

Menorrhagia is the clinical term for menstrual periods with abnormally heavy or prolonged bleeding. While individual flow varies, clinical menorrhagia is defined as menstrual loss exceeding 80 mL per cycle, or bleeding that lasts longer than 7 consecutive days.

Common Indicators You May Have Menorrhagia

  • Soaking through one or more sanitary pads or tampons every hour for several consecutive hours
  • Needing to wake up at night to change menstrual protection
  • Passing blood clots larger than a 1-rupee coin
  • Restricting daily social or work activities due to fear of leaking
  • Experiencing chronic fatigue, breathlessness, or being diagnosed with iron-deficiency anemia

Underlying Clinical Causes

  • Hormonal Imbalance (Anovulatory Cycles): Without regular ovulation, progesterone is not produced, causing the endometrium to overgrow and shed heavily. This is common in PCOS and perimenopause.
  • Uterine Fibroids: Benign muscular tumors of the uterine wall that expand endometrial surface area and disrupt normal contraction of blood vessels.
  • Endometrial Polyps: Small benign growths in the uterine lining that bleed intermittently.
  • Adenomyosis: When endometrial glands grow deep into the muscular uterine wall, leading to an enlarged, heavy, and painful uterus.
  • Thyroid Disorders: Both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism disrupt cycle regularity and bleeding control.

Consult a gynecologist for a pelvic ultrasound scan and complete blood count. Treatment options range from medical therapies (tranexamic acid, hormonal birth control, progesterone IUDs) to targeted minimally invasive procedures.
