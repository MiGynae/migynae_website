White Discharge During Pregnancy: What's Healthy and When to Worry

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MD (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

What is Physiological Leukorrhea?

Noticeable increases in thin, milky-white, or clear vaginal discharge are completely normal and healthy during pregnancy. This discharge—termed leukorrhea—is driven by elevated estrogen levels and increased blood flow to the pelvic region. Its biological purpose is protective: it prevents bacteria from ascending into the uterus and cervix.

Characteristics of Normal, Healthy Discharge

  • Color: Clear, milky white, or creamy ivory
  • Consistency: Thin, mucus-like, or slightly stretchy
  • Odor: Mild, sweetish, or completely odorless

Signs of Vaginal Infection (Yeast or Bacterial Vaginosis)

Hormonal fluctuations alter vaginal pH, making expectant mothers more prone to candidiasis (yeast) and bacterial imbalances. Consult your doctor if your discharge exhibits:

  • Thick, clumpy, white texture resembling curd (paneer), accompanied by intense vulvar itching and redness (classic yeast infection)
  • Greyish-green or yellow frothy discharge with a strong, unpleasant fishy odor
  • Pain or burning sensations during urination or intimacy

Hygiene Tips for Expecting Mothers

Wear breathable cotton underwear, change undergarments if damp, avoid synthetic tight pants, and never use scented chemical intimate washes or douches. Warm water is best for daily external cleansing.
