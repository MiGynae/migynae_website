Low Haemoglobin and Anemia in Pregnancy: Causes, Diet & Iron Therapy

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MD (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Understanding Pregnancy Anemia

During pregnancy, your total blood volume expands by nearly 50% to supply oxygen and nutrients to your developing placenta and fetus. While plasma volume increases dramatically, red blood cell production must catch up—a physiological phenomenon called hemodilution.

Target Haemoglobin (Hb) Levels

The World Health Organization (WHO) and Ministry of Health define anemia in pregnancy as an Hb below 11.0 g/dL in the first and third trimesters, or below 10.5 g/dL in the second trimester.

Common Symptoms of Anemia

  • Persistent, unrefreshing fatigue and weakness
  • Pale skin, lips, or inner eyelids
  • Shortness of breath with minimal exertion
  • Rapid or irregular heartbeat (palpitations)
  • Cold extremities and occasional dizziness

Iron-Rich Indian Nutrition

  • Vegetarian Sources: Spinach (palak), methi, beetroot, lentils (masoor/moong), jaggery (gud in moderation), dates, raisins, and roasted chana.
  • Non-Vegetarian Sources: Well-cooked eggs and poultry provide highly bioavailable heme iron.
  • The Vitamin C Synergy: Iron absorption is significantly amplified when paired with vitamin C. Squeeze fresh lemon juice over your dal or enjoy an amla (Indian gooseberry) with meals.
  • Avoid Calcium Blockers: Do not consume milk, tea, or coffee within 2 hours of taking your iron supplement, as calcium and tannins inhibit iron absorption.
