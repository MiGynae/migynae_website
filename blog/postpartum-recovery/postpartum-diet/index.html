Postpartum Diet: What to Eat After Delivery

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Recover stronger with the right food - MiGynae App

Recovery diet, breastfeeding help and doctor-backed guidance in one place.

What you eat after delivery helps you heal, feel stronger, and feed your baby well. Here is a simple, Indian-friendly guide to eating for recovery.

After delivery your body is healing, making milk, and running low on sleep. The right food gives you energy, speeds recovery, and supports breastfeeding. This is not the time to diet - it is the time to nourish.

Build these into every day

Protein - dal, paneer, eggs, sprouts, chicken or fish. It repairs your body and builds stamina.

Iron - green leafy veg, dates, jaggery. It rebuilds the blood lost at delivery.

Calcium - milk, curd, ragi, til. Important while breastfeeding.

Fibre - vegetables, fruit, whole grains. Keeps digestion easy and prevents constipation.

Warm fluids - water, soups, milk. They support recovery and milk supply.

Indian staples that help

Panjiri / gond laddoo - traditional energy foods, in moderation

Ajwain water and jeera water - may ease digestion and gas

Dahi, dalia, khichdi - light, warm and easy to digest

Enjoy ghee and rich foods in small amounts - your body needs nourishment, not heaviness.

Limit or avoid

Go easy on:

  • Too much ghee or oil
  • Junk food and excess sweets
  • Foods that trigger acidity or gas for you
  • Too much tea, coffee or caffeine
  • Crash diets to lose weight quickly

Eating for your milk supply

If you are breastfeeding, what you eat and drink matters even more. The MiGynae App has breastfeeding tips and foods that support your milk supply - read them alongside this diet.

A few simple tips

Eat small meals often rather than three heavy ones.

Keep water beside you, and sip every time you feed the baby.

Keep healthy snacks within reach for hungry feeding hours.

Weight comes off slowly and naturally - be patient with your body.

Be kind to yourself

Some days you will eat well, some days you will just manage. That is okay. Let family help with cooking so you can rest and feed. A well-fed mother recovers faster.

Healing, not just eating

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the essential nutrients for an Indian postpartum diet?
High-quality protein (dal, paneer, eggs, sprouts, fish) for tissue repair; iron (green vegetables, dates, jaggery) to replenish blood loss; calcium (milk, curd, ragi) for bone density and milk production; and warm fluids for hydration.
Are traditional foods like panjiri and gond laddoos safe after delivery?
Yes, in moderation. These traditional preparations provide concentrated calories, healthy fats, and galactagogues beneficial for nursing mothers. Avoid excessive ghee and sugar which can trigger digestive sluggishness or rapid weight gain.
How many extra calories does a nursing mother need?
Breastfeeding burns significant energy, requiring an additional 450 to 500 nutrient-rich calories per day compared to pre-pregnancy needs, along with at least 2.5 to 3 liters of water daily.
What foods should be avoided or limited after delivery?
Avoid crash dieting, deep-fried or ultra-processed snacks, excessive caffeine (tea/coffee), and gas-producing raw foods if they cause digestive distress or infant colic.
How soon after delivery can I safely start exercising to lose weight?
Gentle walking and pelvic floor (Kegel) exercises can begin as soon as comfortable. Formal abdominal or aerobic workouts should wait until your 6-week postpartum checkup and clearance from your gynaecologist.
