Breastfeeding Tips for New Mothers: The Basics

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MS (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Feed with confidence - MiGynae App

Latch videos, feeding help and doctor-backed guidance in one place.

Breastfeeding is a skill you and your baby learn together. Here is a short, simple start - the full help lives in the app.

Breastfeeding is natural, but it is also learned - by you and your baby together. The first days can feel tricky. Here are the basics to start right; the full help is in the app.

Start right

Try to feed within the first hour after birth.

The first thick yellow milk (colostrum) is gold - small in amount, full of protection. Do not throw it away.

Keep baby skin-to-skin - it calms baby and helps milk flow.

A good latch

Baby's mouth should be wide open, taking in the nipple and much of the areola - not just the tip.

Chin touches the breast, lips flared out.

A good latch should not hurt - if it pinches, gently break the seal and try again.

How often

Feed on demand, usually every 2 to 3 hours, day and night.

Let baby finish one breast before offering the other.

For the first 6 months, breastmilk is all baby needs - no water or top feed unless your doctor advises.

Watch, don't just read

Feeding is far easier once you have seen it. The MiGynae App has short videos - how to hold your baby and breastfeeding positions - so you can get comfortable and latch right. Watch them in the app.

Is baby getting enough?

Good signs: 6 or more wet nappies a day, steady weight gain, you can hear swallowing, and baby seems settled after feeds. If baby is very sleepy, not feeding, or has few wet nappies, see your doctor.

Look after yourself too

Drink water each time you feed, and eat well.

Rest when you can - tiredness affects milk.

Sore or cracked nipples and hard, full breasts are common early on and can be managed - do not suffer in silence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is feeding colostrum in the first hour essential?
Colostrum is the thick, nutrient-dense first milk packed with maternal antibodies (IgA), proteins, and gut-protective factors that coat the baby's digestive tract, act as a natural laxative to clear meconium, and provide early immune defense.
How can I tell if my baby has a good breastfeeding latch?
The baby's mouth should be wide open taking in the nipple and a generous amount of the lower areola. The chin touches the breast, lips are flared outward (not curled inward), and feeding does not cause sharp pinching pain after the initial latch.
How often should a newborn feed?
Newborns should be fed on demand whenever showing early hunger cues (rooting, lip smacking, hands to mouth), typically 8 to 12 times in 24 hours (every 2 to 3 hours), including during the night.
What are reliable signs that baby is getting enough milk?
At least 6 heavy wet diapers per day after day 4, 3–4 soft yellow bowel movements, visible/audible swallowing during nursing, content behavior after feeds, and consistent weight gain verified by your paediatrician.
What should I do for sore or cracked nipples?
Ensure the latch is deep rather than shallow on the nipple tip. Express a few drops of colostrum or breast milk and rub gently onto the nipple to air-dry. If pain persists, consult an IBCLC lactation consultant or gynaecologist.
