Travelling During Pregnancy: Safe Flying, Car Trips & India Train Travel

Medically reviewed by Dr. Shuchita Batra, MBBS, MD (Obstetrics & Gynaecology)

Is it Safe to Travel While Pregnant?

In an uncomplicated, healthy pregnancy, travel is generally very safe when planned thoughtfully. The golden window for travel is the second trimester (weeks 14 to 28), when early morning sickness has subsided and energy levels are high, while the physical bulk of the third trimester has not yet peaked.

Air Travel Guidelines

  • Airline Policies: Most commercial Indian airlines (such as IndiGo and Air India) allow unrestricted flying up to week 28. Between weeks 28 and 36, a medical certificate (Fit to Fly) from your obstetrician is mandatory. Flying is typically prohibited after 36 weeks.
  • Hydration and Circulation: Cabin air is exceptionally dry. Sip water constantly and perform simple calf flexes every 30 minutes to reduce the risk of Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT).
  • Wear Compression Socks: Graduated knee-high compression stockings reduce ankle swelling during flights exceeding 2 hours.

Car and Road Travel Precautions

  • Proper Seatbelt Placement: Never position the lap belt across your pregnant belly. Place the lower strap low across your hips and pelvic bone, beneath your bump. The diagonal shoulder strap should rest securely across your chest between your breasts.
  • Frequent Breaks: On highway drives, pause every 90 minutes to stretch, walk, and use clean restroom facilities.
  • Avoid Rough Roads: Excessive jerking on unpaved or potholed roads can provoke abdominal discomfort or uterine contractions.

Train Travel Across India

Train travel offers ample room to recline and walk. Request lower berths in AC coaches, carry clean bottled water, pack home-prepared meals to avoid gastrointestinal infections, and travel accompanied.
